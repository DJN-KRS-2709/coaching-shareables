Enterprise strategy · 7 Sep 2026

Package,
don't customize.

Package the curriculum. Customize the delivery. Enterprise doesn't need a content studio, it needs a distribution channel for content B2C has already proven, wrapped in an instructor who understands the client's business.

Dejan Krstic For Carlos, Fernando, Lisa Prep for Mon/Tue sessions & 15 Sep board
I

The strategy

One library. Two motions.

Product School has one product: the content library. B2C and enterprise are two ways of selling it, not two things to build. B2C is where content gets proven, at volume, with data attached. Enterprise is where proven content gets distributed at a premium.

The moment enterprise starts building its own curriculum, it stops being a channel and becomes a competing studio with a worse feedback loop. That is the position we are in today.

Three principles, all at once

The container is fixed. The contents move.

We sell 24 hours, two certifications, one price band. What goes inside is drawn from a governed pool and chosen during discovery. The client experiences that pick as design work done for them. It costs us nothing, because everything in the pool is already built.

The custom layer is the instructor, not the curriculum.

What a client experiences as bespoke is an instructor fluent in their business. That is a briefing pack, not a rebuilt course. Curriculum customization is expensive and unrepeatable, and it is where delivery quality most often slips.

Only sell what we can deliver twice.

A certification enters the pool when two or more instructors have taught it above a 4.0 curriculum rating. If exactly one person can teach it, it is not a product yet. It is a dependency.

II

The plan

Sell where the team is, not what we have

An enterprise buyer doesn't ask which certifications we offer. They say where their team is today. Three rungs, each 24 hours, each assembled from certifications that already exist. The top rung branches by audience.

24h

Vibe Coding + Claude Code for PMs

Broadest audience rung · Swaps: Product Management for non-tech orgs

24h

Proven with a global bank · Highest margin and differentiation

3b · Advanced AI: Governance

24h

AI Evals + AI Product Strategy

"Our regulator expects effective challenge on models we can't yet explain."

Name it in regulator language, not practitioner language

Bridge picks are legitimate. Product Management plus AI Product Management crosses two rungs, and for a mid-maturity non-tech enterprise it is probably the most sellable combination in the catalog. Treat the rungs as shelves, not walls.

The ladder is the expansion motion enterprise has never had. Land Fluency at a lower price, the easier first yes for an account that has never bought from us. Come back for Advanced once the first cohort finishes. That is a second and third sale from one account without writing a single new module.

The one hard rule

Swap whole certifications. Never cherry-pick modules. The library already says so: all ten certifications are marked standalone: false with an explicit must_travel_with block, because the learning arc breaks when you pull modules out of it.

Swapping certifications is packaging. Cherry-picking modules is what our refund cases have in common.

The delivery layer decides whether any of this lands

Content is half a package. The other half is everything wrapped around it. B2C has already built almost all of it, and enterprise keeps rebuilding it deal by deal.

AssetStatusWhat's missing for enterprise
Pre-course material Partial Pre-reads exist for all 60 modules, but only Vibe Coding has prework as a formal contract with an observation plan. For the other nine, nobody tracks completion.
Setup Partial Corporate IT is a different problem than a consumer installing a tool. Needs a pre-engagement IT checklist. A plausible hidden driver of enterprise dropout.
Instructor guidance Built 60 instructor guides at full coverage, with speaker notes and timing. It exists and is not being reused. A recent enterprise program had a parallel spec written from scratch, answering a question B2C had already answered sixty times.
Instructor knows the company Missing Exists nowhere. Client knowledge lives in the sales conversation and dies there. This is the one genuinely new thing to build, and it is the cheapest.

The new asset: a client briefing pack

One or two pages per engagement, written once, reused across every cohort and every module: what the company does and how it makes money, who is in the room and what they use daily, their buying reason in their own words from the sales call, the five internal terms that make an instructor sound like an insider, their systems and data constraints, three lab scenarios from their real work, what leadership will judge this on, and what not to say.

This is the honest version of customization. The content stays ours and stays stable. The instructor's fluency in the client's world is what the client actually experiences as bespoke.

With AI Agents +503%, Responsible AI +495%, RAG +275%. Meanwhile ChatGPT, chatbot and conversational AI all fell. Demand is moving up the ladder, not down it. Dice, 7M+ postings via Lightcast, Aug 2026

$4,200 Maven's per-seat evals price

For 12 to 20 hours, and it is their highest-grossing course. Our top certification is $2,999 for more contact time. There is pricing headroom at the top rung.

6% Companies with no AI training at all

The literacy tier is saturated. Large banks have typically already put most of their staff through an internal AI literacy program. You cannot sell a bank the rung it already built. TalentLMS 2026

47% AI product roles at manager level
